The Trigonometric Origin

Every point on a circle can be viewed as the tip of a right triangle.

The Opposite side represents the vertical displacement ($y$), while the Adjacent side represents the horizontal displacement ($x$). As the angle changes, the lengths of these sides oscillate predictably.

Parametric Synthesis

The parametric equation treats $x$ and $y$ as independent functions of a single variable: the angle. By calculating these components separately, we can plot a perfect circle without needing a physical center-point tether.

Wave Components

Sine & Cosine Oscillation

X: The Horizontal Leg

The Adjacent side of the triangle. Its length is determined by the Cosine function, representing how far left or right a point sits from the origin.

Y: The Vertical Leg

The Opposite side of the triangle. Its length is determined by the Sine function, representing how far forward or backward a point sits from the origin.
